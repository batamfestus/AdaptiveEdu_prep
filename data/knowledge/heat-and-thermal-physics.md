topic: Heat and Thermal Physics

## Use specific and latent heat
Heat needed to change temperature: $Q = mc\Delta\theta$, where $c$ is the specific heat capacity (J/kg/°C). Heat needed to change state at constant temperature: $Q = mL$, where $L$ is the specific latent heat of fusion or vaporisation. For water, $c \approx 4200\ J/kg/°C$.

Worked example: raising 2 kg of water by 10 °C needs $Q = 2 \times 4200 \times 10 = 84000\ J$.

Common mistakes: using $Q = mc\Delta\theta$ while a substance is changing state (its temperature does not change then), forgetting to use kilograms, and confusing heat with temperature.

## Apply gas laws
Boyle's law: at constant temperature $pV$ is constant ($p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$). Charles' law: at constant pressure $V/T$ is constant. Pressure law: at constant volume $p/T$ is constant. Combined: $p_1V_1/T_1 = p_2V_2/T_2$. Temperatures must be in kelvin: $T(K) = \theta(°C) + 273$.

Worked example: 2 litres of gas at 100 kPa is compressed to 1 litre at constant temperature, so the pressure becomes 200 kPa.

Common mistake: using temperatures in °C instead of kelvin.
