topic: Waves, Sound and Light

## Use wave quantities
Wave speed $v = f\lambda$, and the period $T = 1/f$. In a transverse wave the particles vibrate at right angles to the direction of travel (light, water ripples). In a longitudinal wave they vibrate along it (sound). Amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position. Wavelength is the distance between successive crests.

Worked example: $f = 50\ Hz$ and $\lambda = 4\ m$ give $v = 200\ m/s$.

Sound needs a material medium; light does not. When a wave enters a new medium its frequency stays the same, while its speed and wavelength change.

Common mistakes: confusing frequency with speed, and saying the frequency changes when a wave changes medium.

## Apply reflection and refraction
Reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal. A plane mirror forms a virtual image, the same size as the object and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

Refraction: light bends when its speed changes. Entering a denser medium it bends towards the normal. Refractive index $n = \sin i / \sin r = c/v$. Total internal reflection happens when light travels from a denser to a less dense medium at an angle greater than the critical angle $C$, where $\sin C = 1/n$. Lenses: $1/f = 1/u + 1/v$.

Worked example: for $n = 1.5$, $\sin C = 0.667$, so $C \approx 42°$.

Common mistake: measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
