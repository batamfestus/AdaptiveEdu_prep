topic: Newton's Laws and Momentum

## Apply Newton's laws of motion
First law: an object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it (inertia). Second law: the resultant force $F = ma$. Third law: if A pushes on B, B pushes back on A with an equal and opposite force, and these two forces act on different objects. Weight $W = mg$.

Worked example: a resultant force of 20 N on a 4 kg body gives $a = 20/4 = 5\ m/s^2$.

Common mistakes: using one force instead of the resultant force, thinking an action-reaction pair acts on the same object, and confusing mass (kg) with weight (N).

## Use conservation of momentum
Momentum $p = mv$ (kg m/s) is a vector. When no external resultant force acts, the total momentum of a system stays constant: $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$. If the bodies stick together, they move with a common velocity $(m_1 + m_2)v$. Impulse = $Ft$ = change in momentum.

Worked example: a 2 kg trolley at 3 m/s sticks to a stationary 1 kg trolley: $6 = 3v$, so $v = 2\ m/s$. In recoil (a gun and bullet) the total momentum is zero, so they move in opposite directions.

Common mistakes: ignoring direction (signs), and assuming kinetic energy is conserved in every collision (only in elastic ones).
