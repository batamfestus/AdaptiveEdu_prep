topic: Electromagnetism and Modern Physics

## Explain magnetic effects of current
A current in a wire produces a magnetic field around it (the right-hand grip rule gives its direction). A solenoid has a field like a bar magnet, stronger with more current, more turns, or a soft iron core. A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force $F = BIL$ (at right angles), with direction given by Fleming's left-hand rule: this is the motor effect.

Electromagnetic induction: a changing magnetic flux induces an e.m.f. (Faraday's law), in a direction that opposes the change (Lenz's law). Transformer: $V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p$. A step-up transformer raises the voltage and lowers the current; an ideal one has $V_pI_p = V_sI_s$. Transformers need alternating current.

Common mistakes: using the wrong hand rule, and thinking a magnet at rest inside a coil induces a current (the flux must be changing).

## Describe atomic and nuclear processes
The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. The atomic number $Z$ is the number of protons; the mass number $A$ is the number of protons plus neutrons. Isotopes have the same $Z$ but different $A$.

Alpha particles are helium nuclei (stopped by paper, strongly ionising). Beta particles are fast electrons (stopped by a few mm of aluminium). Gamma rays are electromagnetic waves (reduced by thick lead). Alpha decay lowers $A$ by 4 and $Z$ by 2. Beta decay raises $Z$ by 1 and leaves $A$ unchanged.

Half-life is the time for the activity to halve. After $n$ half-lives, $(1/2)^n$ remains. Worked example: 80 g with a half-life of 3 days leaves 10 g after 9 days (3 half-lives).

Photoelectric effect: photon energy $E = hf$. Electrons are emitted only if $f$ is at least the threshold frequency. Brighter light releases more electrons, not faster ones.

Common mistakes: thinking the half-life is half of the total lifetime, and thinking brighter light gives the electrons more energy.
