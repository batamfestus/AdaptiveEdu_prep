topic: Work, Energy and Power

## Calculate work, energy and power
Work $W = Fs$ (force times distance moved in the direction of the force), measured in joules. Kinetic energy $= \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Gravitational potential energy $= mgh$. Power = work / time = energy / time (watts); also $P = Fv$.

Worked example: a machine does 600 J of work in 20 s, so its power is $600/20 = 30\ W$.

Common mistakes: using a force that is not along the direction of motion (a force perpendicular to the motion does no work), and confusing energy (J) with power (W).

## Apply conservation of energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. For a falling object with no air resistance, the potential energy lost equals the kinetic energy gained: $mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh}$. Efficiency = (useful output energy / total input energy) $\times$ 100%.

Worked example: dropped from rest through 5 m with $g = 10\ m/s^2$: $v = \sqrt{2 \times 10 \times 5} = 10\ m/s$.

Common mistakes: forgetting the energy lost as heat and sound, and giving an efficiency above 100%.
