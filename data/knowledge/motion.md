topic: Motion

## Distinguish scalars and vectors
Scalars have magnitude only: distance, speed, mass, time, energy, temperature. Vectors have magnitude and direction: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum.

Distance is the total length of the path travelled. Displacement is the straight-line change in position, with direction. Speed = distance / time. Velocity = displacement / time.

Worked example: a runner completes one lap of a 400 m track. Distance = 400 m, displacement = 0 m, so the average velocity is 0.

Common mistake: assuming displacement always equals distance.

## Apply equations of uniform acceleration
For constant acceleration $a$, initial velocity $u$, final velocity $v$, time $t$ and displacement $s$: $v = u + at$, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$, $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, $s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t$.

Starting from rest means $u = 0$. A slowing object has negative acceleration. Free fall uses $a = g$; $g = 10\ m/s^2$ is often used in JAMB-style questions, but always use the value the question gives.

Worked example: from rest with $a = 2\ m/s^2$ for 5 s: $s = \frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 5^2 = 25\ m$ and $v = 10\ m/s$.

Common mistakes: using these equations when acceleration is not constant, mixing units, and sign errors.

## Interpret motion graphs
On a displacement-time graph the gradient is the velocity; a horizontal line means the object is at rest. On a velocity-time graph the gradient is the acceleration, a horizontal line is constant velocity, and the area under the graph is the displacement.

Worked example: a velocity-time graph rises from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s then stays flat for 5 s. Area = $\frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 10 + 5 \times 10 = 75\ m$.

Common mistakes: reading a velocity-time graph as the shape of the path, and confusing the gradient with the area.
